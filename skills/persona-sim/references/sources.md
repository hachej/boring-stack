# Sources and limits

These works motivate persona conditioning and controls on simulation bias. They do not validate this skill's fictional people as representative humans. Cite the source relevant to a claim rather than treating this list as a general endorsement of synthetic research.

| Source | What it contributes | Limit to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| Argyle et al., 2023, [Out of One, Many: Using Language Models to Simulate Human Samples](https://doi.org/10.1017/pan.2023.2) | Backstory-conditioned "silicon samples" | Political-domain results do not establish validity for product conversations |
| Aher, Arriaga, and Kalai, 2023, [Using Large Language Models to Simulate Multiple Humans and Replicate Human Subject Studies](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/aher23a.html) | Behavioral replications and model-specific distortions, including hyper-accuracy | A capable simulator may behave unlike a person with bounded knowledge |
| Brand, Israeli, and Ngwe, [Using GPT for market research](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4395751), later titled Using LLMs for Market Research | Synthetic preferences and choices in market-research tasks | Plausible prose is insufficient. Check choices across turns and do not generalize beyond tested tasks |
| Park et al., 2024 version, [Generative Agent Simulations of 1,000 People](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109v1) | Individual simulations grounded in two-hour interviews | Invented incident histories are much weaker evidence and must not be called interview-grounded |
| Ge et al., 2024, [Scaling Synthetic Data Creation with 1,000,000,000 Personas](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.20094), [PersonaHub collection](https://github.com/tencent-ailab/persona-hub) | Broad persona-conditioned synthesis | Scale and variety do not establish fidelity to individual humans |
| Yao et al., 2024, [Tau-bench: A Benchmark for Tool-Agent-User Interaction in Real-World Domains](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045) | Open-ended simulated users and database-state outcome checks | Separate player perception from recorded execution and policy compliance |
| [How Far Can We Go with Synthetic User Experience Research?](https://interactions.acm.org/archive/view/may-june-2024/how-far-can-we-go-with-synthetic-user-experience-research), 2024 | Cautions about synthetic user-experience research | Synthetic participants cannot replace evidence from actual users |
| [Concerns on Bias when Creating Synthetic Personae](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05080), 2024 | Bias risks in generated personas | Concrete fictional incidents avoid some caricature, not all model bias |
| [Lost in Simulation: LLM-Simulated Users are Unreliable Proxies for Human Users in Agentic Evaluations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17087), 2026 | Simulator-dependent scores and population-dependent miscalibration | Compare matched cases across players. A higher agent score does not make its player a better human proxy |

## Operational lessons behind this skill

The originating fictional PM experiments exposed dropped terminal acceptance, early perceived success, budget loss from interrupted calls, uneven role coverage, and evaluator false positives. A later frozen comparison found lower token and question counts without a demonstrated experience improvement. The skill therefore requires final-message delivery, a cumulative reservation ledger, interleaved scheduling, evidence audits, and separate outcome and perception reporting.

Examples of evaluator defects included source facts missing from the comparator, future intent mistaken for completion, grouping language mistaken for a deadline, and declarative "who ... and what ..." clauses rejected as questions. These are reasons to test the evaluator, not instructions for an agent to avoid normal language or evade genuine checks.

Held-out findings may guide a later version. They must not be used to revise the frozen version and then reported as an independent held-out success. A targeted repeat of an observed failure is diagnostic evidence, even with a fresh transcript.

Research references remain external reading, not runtime dependencies. Do not publish real interviews, personal data, or unpublished product plans with example profiles or transcripts. Claims about real-user representativeness require a separate human study and appropriate consent.
